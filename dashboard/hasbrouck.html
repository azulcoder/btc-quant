EVERY SERIES ON THIS PAGE IS SIMULATED. No real market data was read to build it: no data/, no hf://, no tick store, no LockBox. Each panel states the truth that was planted before the estimator ran, so agreement can be judged rather than taken. This page adds zero to the look counter.

Model, in log price (1e-4 = 1 bps), t in trade time:

m_t  = m_{t-1} + w_t          efficient price, a random walk
w_t  = lambda*q_t + u_t       trades carry information
p_t  = m_t + c*q_t            observed transaction price
dp_t = -c*q_{t-1} + (c+lambda)*q_t + u_t

gamma_0 = c^2 + s^2 + su^2,  gamma_1 = -c*s,  gamma_k = 0 (k >= 2),  s := c + lambda
sigma2_w = lambda^2 + su^2 = gamma_0 + 2*gamma_1     <- identified; c, lambda, su^2 are NOT

Reading

Three independent routes and one algebraic duplicate land within 0.56% of a value none of them was told.

That is the evidence for the implementation. Each route uses a different part of the series: E2/E3 the first two autocovariances, E4 the variance of long-horizon differences, E9 a 30-lag autoregression. A coding error would have to be shared by all three to survive this.

The honest correction to the headline: E2 and E3 are not independent. They differ by 0.00e+00 here, and over 20,000 random admissible moment pairs the worst relative gap is 2.0e-12 [DIUKUR] — a machine-precision identity, not an agreement. For an MA(1) the two Wold roots satisfy s2_a + s2_b = gamma_0 and s2_a*s2_b = gamma_1^2, so theta(1)^2*sigma2_eps collapses to gamma_0 + 2*gamma_1 exactly.

So this panel shows three independent routes, not four. E3 earns its place by carrying the invertibility choice and by abstaining where E2's gate would not fire, not by being a second opinion.

Reading

c is identified only up to [3.7963, 5.2995] bps. The planted 4.000 bps is inside: yes [DIUKUR].

A point estimate of c is a point chosen by an assumption. The Roll number is the point the assumption lambda = 0 picks out; the E5 bound is the point sigma2_u = 0 picks out. Nothing in (gamma_0, gamma_1) can choose between them, because both ends generate the same two moments.

Roll is the geometric mean of c and c+lambda. gamma_1 = -c*(c+lambda), so sqrt(-gamma_1) = sqrt(c*(c+lambda)) = 5.2915 bps against the measured 5.2995 bps [DIUKUR]. By AM-GM it sits between its two terms, so it overstates the half-spread and understates the full spread — one number wrong in two directions at once.

The interval costs nothing: both ends come from gamma_0 and gamma_1, which were computed anyway. Reporting the point instead of the interval buys precision that is not there.

Not calibrated to BTCUSDT. These planted values are the VERIFY §4 fixtures. This repo's own measurement puts the median perp spread at one tick, 0.0157 bps [DIUKUR] — around 400x tighter, and a regime where discretisation binds for the whole Roll family (VERIFY §6). The interval shown here is a property of the estimator, not a claim about the instrument.

The mechanism, in closed form: under an MA(1) the sample autocorrelations at lags 2..10 are correlated — Bartlett gives adjacent correlation 2*rho_1/(1+2*rho_1^2) = -0.611 at rho_1 = -0.4058, and 0.124 two lags apart. Summing their squares as if they were independent keeps the mean (tr(R) = 9) but inflates the variance to 2*tr(R^2) = 30.4. Measured: 26.1 ± 2.4 [DIUKUR] — within 1.8 SE of the prediction and 3.4 SE from chi2(9)'s 18.0. The rejection rate is the blunter and more direct measurement, and it is -0.9 SE above nominal: the chi2(9) 95% point 16.90 sits too low, so the gate refuses about 4% of the series it should accept.

Reading

On a true MA(3), gamma_0 + 2*gamma_1 is out by 1.06x, 0.74x, 289.0x and -0.59x — the last one a negative variance.

The refusal is what makes that legible. Every one of these four returns ABSTAIN when the gate is left on; the numbers above exist only because force=True switched it off, which is a test affordance and never a production path.

Two failure modes, and only one of them is loud. The alternating pattern returns -1.7242e-09, a negative variance, which cannot be missed. The all-negative pattern returns a small, positive, plausible 7.2100e-09 that is wrong by a factor of 289.0x. Nothing about that number looks wrong.

The gate's own error rate is 4.0%, not the 5% it advertises.

The direction is safe — it refuses work it could have done, rather than passing work it should not — and E4 and E9 need no gate at all, so nothing is stranded. But a 5% label on an 4% test is a claim without a checker, and it now has one.

Reading

A formula that is wrong twice returns the right number at theta = 0.618034.

1/(theta*(1+theta)) equals 1 when theta^2 + theta - 1 = 0, whose invertible root is the golden-ratio conjugate. At that point sigma2_eps/sum(phi) — which drops the square and reads phi(1) as sum(phi) instead of 1 - sum(phi) — agrees with the truth to within the sampling error of the correct formula.

This has no practical use. It has a rhetorical one, and it is the whole argument for positive controls: "the number came out plausible" is evidence about nothing. A single-point check at the wrong theta would have certified this implementation.

The rest of the map matters for a different reason. sigma2_eps/sum(phi) is negative for every theta < 0 here, which is the bounce-driven case, so the bug looks like it announces itself. It does not reliably: VERIFY §5 measures it staying positive on 27.5% of seeds at theta = -0.01. That is why sigma2_w_ar checks phi(1) > 0 explicitly instead of waiting for a negative number to appear.

The alternative to ABSTAIN is not a missing number, it is a wrong one. Setting c = 0 on the abstaining seeds would report "no spread" on a series that has none — right by accident, since the same reflex reports "no spread" whenever gamma_1 happens to come out positive on a series that does have one. Taking sqrt(|gamma_1|) instead fabricates a median spread of 0.223 bps out of a series with no spread at all. Both are silent; neither would fail a test that only checks the output is a number.

The estimators that do not need a sign stay honest on the same random walk: E3 returns theta = +0.00449 and sigma2_w/sigma2_eps = 1.00899 against a true 1.0 [DIUKUR], and E4's intercept is 4.0208e-08 against the planted 4.0000e-08. E4's slope averages -1.69e-09 with sd 9.42e-09 across 20 seeds — indistinguishable from zero, and a reminder of why the module labels it slope_DIAGNOSTIC_ONLY and refuses to derive c from it.

identified_interval_c refuses the momentum series for the same reason Roll does — ABSTAIN gamma_1 >= 0 — no spread to bound. E5 does not: pricing_error_lower_bound returns OK 6.6131e-09 there. That is defensible arithmetic — the bound is on the Wold pricing error, which exists for any covariance-stationary series and does not need a spread to be defined — but on a trending series the number is not a half-spread and nothing should read it as one. Three functions over the same two moments, two refusing and one answering, is worth knowing before any of them is pointed at a tape.

E5's own negative control passes cleanly: on the pure random walk the bound is 8.032e-13, which is 2.0e-05 of sigma_u^2 — zero to five decimal places, as it must be when there is no pricing error to bound [DIUKUR].

Reading

On a series with no spread, the Roll estimator refuses 51.0% of the time and never reports a spread of zero.

Roughly half is the correct answer, not a shortfall: with c = 0 the sample gamma_1 is a coin flip about zero, so half the seeds have no real sqrt(-gamma_1). RULE-EXTRACT-2 makes that half a refusal with a reason attached.

The momentum control is the sharper one. Positive first-order autocovariance is a momentum signature, and an estimator that returned sqrt(|gamma_1|) there would report a spread with the wrong sign of evidence entirely — reading trend as friction.

An abstention is a result. It is the only output here that separates "nothing is there" from "nothing was measurable", which is blindness class B and the thing a silent zero destroys.

Two routes to the same limit, from the same fit: summing the MA coefficients to k = 20 gives 0.487697, and 1/phi(1) — the limit of that sum — gives 0.487010. They differ by +0.14%, which is the truncation left in a 20-step sum, not disagreement.

Reading

The price level moves 2.05x its permanent displacement on impact, then gives most of it back.

This is the LEVEL response, not the response of the price change: the cumulative sum of the MA coefficients, which is the object RULE-EXTRACT-3 points at. It starts at 1.00 by construction and settles at theta(1) = 0.4877 [DISIMPULKAN]. The gap between the two is the transient part — the half-spread paid and then reversed.

The whole transient is one step, and that is a property of the fixture, not a finding. The planted process is MA(1), so all of the reversal lands at k = 1 and the path is flat afterwards — which is exactly why the flat part is a useful check: the AR(30) fit recovers coefficients that are near zero past lag 1 without being told the order. Real order flow is autocorrelated, so a real path would decay over many trades instead.

What this is not. The AR here is univariate, fitted on Delta p alone, so the response is to a Wold innovation and not to a trade. Attributing any part of it to order flow needs the price-trade VAR (E6), which is not implemented — see the footer. Reading a single coefficient of any such VAR as lambda is forbidden by RULE-EXTRACT-3 in the first place; the cumulative path is what the rule points to instead.

G. What this page is, and what it is not

Everything above is simulated. Every series was generated in-process from a seeded RNG with known parameters. No real market data was touched: this script has no data access, reads no partition, opens no database, and makes no network call. It adds zero to the look counter, because a look is a specification evaluated against real data and nothing here has seen any.

RULE-EXTRACT-5 stands. No estimator in this module may touch a real partition before a pre-registration declares the whole specification set with an explicit N_trials cap. The choices that would need declaring are already known and are not cosmetic: the AR truncation order K, the horizon grid and k_min for E4, the time basis (event vs calendar), the treatment of funding windows at 00:00/08:00/16:00 UTC, the subsample unit, the handling of days carrying a recorded-damage entry, and the sample window. Choosing any of them after seeing a result is an unrecorded look.

Not implemented

  • E6 — the price-trade VAR (lambda from the cumulative impulse response, the variance decomposition, R^2_w). Nothing on this page estimates lambda. Panel F's response is univariate, to a Wold innovation, and cannot attribute anything to order flow.
  • E7 — VECM and information share. No cointegration, no error correction, no information-share bounds. The perp-vs-spot question this repo cares about is not touched.
  • E8 — the GMM / MA(q) moment estimator, deliberately: 2^q parameter sets share the same autocovariances and only one is invertible, and E9 reaches sigma2_w without knowing the MA order.
  • RULE-EXTRACT-9's required test — that sum_j theta_j*Omega*theta_j' and theta(1)*Omega*theta(1)' are different numbers — cannot exist until E6 does, because both sides need a VAR.
  • The book sections never extracted at all: §5–§6 (sequential and strategic trade models, Glosten-Milgrom and Kyle), §8.e–§8.h (smoothing and filtering approaches to sigma2_s), §10–§11 (inventory control; invertibility and Wold revisited), §14 (structural models: Glosten-Harris, MRR, Huang-Stoll), §15 (PIN), §16 (what the asymmetric-information measures actually measure), §18–§21 (limit orders, uncertain execution, dynamic equilibrium), §22 (asset pricing with transaction costs, Amihud-style liquidity measures), and the 2003 US-equity market-structure appendix.

Two caveats that outlive this page

  • Attribution is unverified. The source PDF is not on this machine, so every section-number citation carried by the extraction documents is [UNVERIFIED]. The mathematics on this page stands on its own derivations and these simulations; the attribution does not stand at all until the source can be read.
  • Simulation proves the formula, not its application. Every series here has iid trade direction and homoskedastic Gaussian innovations. Real order flow has neither — direction is autocorrelated (order splitting) and volatility varies within the day. What these panels establish is that the algebra and the implementation are right. Whether the assumptions hold on any real tape is a separate question, and answering it costs a look.
